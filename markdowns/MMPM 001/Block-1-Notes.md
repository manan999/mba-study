### UNIT 1: CONSUMER BEHAVIOUR — NATURE, SCOPE, MODELS AND APPLICATIONS

#### 1. Nature and Scope of Consumer Behaviour
*   **Definition:** Consumer behaviour is the decision process and physical activity engaged in when evaluating, acquiring, using, or disposing of goods and services. It includes the mental and emotional responses that precede, accompany, and follow these activities.
*   **Scope:** The scope of consumer behaviour can be understood through Loudon and Della Bitta's framework, which comprises three main sections:
    1.  *The Decision Process:* Problem recognition, information search, alternative evaluation, purchase, and post-purchase evaluation.
    2.  *Individual Determinants:* Motivation and involvement, attitudes, personality and self-concept, learning and memory, and information processing.
    3.  *External Environment / Group Influences:* Culture, sub-culture, social class, social groups, family, and personal influences.
*   **Key Roles in Buying Situations:**
    *   *Initiator:* Determines that a need or want is not being met.
    *   *Influencer:* Intentionally or unintentionally guides the buying decision.
    *   *Buyer:* Performs the actual physical transaction.
    *   *User:* Consumes or uses the product/service.

#### 2. Models of Consumer Behaviour
*   **Howard-Sheth Model:** Explains decision-making under three levels of problem-solving:
    1.  *Extensive Problem Solving:* Buyer has no basic info about brands/preferences and seeks detailed information.
    2.  *Limited Problem Solving:* Buyer has partial knowledge and seeks comparative brand info.
    3.  *Routinised Response Behaviour:* Buyer has full knowledge, easily differentiates brands, and makes quick replenishment purchases.
    *   *Variables:* Inputs (stimuli like price, quality, family/social class), Perceptual and Learning Constructs (motives, attitudes, perceptual bias, stimulus ambiguity), Outputs (purchase decision, satisfaction), and Exogenous Variables (importance of purchase, time pressure).
*   **Engel-Blackwell-Miniard (EBM) Model:** A comprehensive cognitive processing model with five core components:
    1.  *Information Input:* Marketer stimuli (ads, displays) and non-market stimuli (family, reviews).
    2.  *Information Processing:* Exposure, attention, comprehension, acceptance, and retention (using memory).
    3.  *Decision Process:* Problem recognition $\to$ Search for alternatives $\to$ Alternative evaluation $\to$ Choice $\to$ Post-purchase outcomes.
    4.  *Decision Process Variables:* Demographics, motives, beliefs, attitude, values, and lifestyle.
    5.  *External Influences:* Culture, social class, family, and reference groups.

#### 3. Online Consumer Behaviour & Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
*   **Online Buying Process vs. Physical Store Buying:**
    *   *Information Access:* Online shopping provides ubiquitous, 24/7 access to information, peer reviews, and price comparison databases from anywhere, whereas physical shopping requires visiting retail locations.
    *   *Convenience:* Online shopping minimizes physical effort and time (buying in transit/multitasking), while physical stores allow sensory touch-and-feel of products before purchasing.
    *   *Payment & Delivery:* Online requires digital wallets, COD, or credit cards followed by a delivery lag. Physical stores offer immediate possession and cash exchange.
*   **Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) by Fred Davis:** Explains a consumer's propensity to buy online based on two key mental constructs:
    1.  *Perceived Usefulness (PU):* The extent to which a consumer believes online shopping will save time and improve transaction efficiency.
    2.  *Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU):* How simple, user-friendly, and convenient the online shopping platform is to navigate.
    *   *Outcome:* Both PU and PEOU shape the user's *Attitude* and *Behavioral Intention* to use the technology, ultimately leading to the *Actual Use* of online platforms.
    *   *Influencing Factors:* External variables (service quality, payment security, delivery speed, reviews) and Individual variables (prior digital experience, computer literacy, time pressure).
    *   *Example:* A busy professional needs to replace a water geyser. Instead of visiting multiple dealers, they browse e-commerce apps on their phone, check specs and user ratings (PEOU), see a discount and free next-day installation (PU), pay via a digital wallet, and complete the purchase in 15 minutes.

#### 4. Marketing Applications of Consumer Behaviour
1.  **Analyzing Market Opportunities:** Identifying unfulfilled customer needs by tracking lifestyle trends (e.g., rise in working women driving demand for timesaving home appliances like microwave ovens and dishwashers).
2.  **Target Market Selection:** Segmenting the market based on buyer traits and focusing resources on high-potential groups (e.g., launching small shampoo sachets at lower price points to target low-income rural consumers who wash hair occasionally).
3.  **Product Mix Decisions:** Guiding product attributes, sizes, and packaging (e.g., Maggi introducing regional flavors; brands shifting from rigid tins to zip-lock pouches to prevent spillage).
4.  **Pricing Decisions:** Understanding price sensitivity, price-quality associations, and consumer perceptions of discounts.
5.  **Distribution Strategy:** Deciding the channel format (e.g., direct-to-home personal selling for new product concepts like vacuum cleaners, where customer education and live demos are essential).
6.  **Promotion Strategy:** Matching the media (digital, print, TV) to the target segment's habits.
7.  **Non-Profit & Social Marketing:** Redesigning public service approaches (e.g., tax authorities running friendly awareness campaigns to improve their public image).

### UNIT 2: CONSUMER BEHAVIOUR AND LIFESTYLE MARKETING

#### 1. Concept of Lifestyle and Psychographics
*   **Lifestyle:** A person's pattern of living in the world as expressed in their activities, interests, and opinions. It reflects how individuals spend their time and money.
*   **Psychographics:** The quantitative technique used to measure lifestyles and develop lifestyle profiles of consumers.
*   **AIO Inventories (Activities, Interests, and Opinions):** The primary tool used to measure psychographics by asking consumers to respond to a series of statements:
    *   *Activities:* Work, hobbies, social events, vacation, entertainment, shopping.
    *   *Interests:* Family, home, job, community, recreation, fashion, food.
    *   *Opinions:* Themselves, social issues, politics, business, economics, education, products.

#### 2. The VALS (Values and Lifestyles) Classification
Developed by Arnold Mitchell (SRI International), VALS segment adults based on psychological traits (self-orientation) and resources.
*   **Three Self-Orientations:**
    1.  *Principles-Oriented:* Choices are guided by internal beliefs and values rather than others' opinions.
    2.  *Status-Oriented:* Choices are heavily influenced by the actions, approval, and opinions of peers.
    3.  *Action-Oriented:* Motivated by a desire for social/physical activity, variety, adventure, and risk.
*   **Resources:** Range from high to low (income, education, self-confidence, health, eagerness to buy).
*   **Eight VALS-2 Lifestyle Segments:**
    1.  *Innovators (formerly Actualizers):* High resources, high self-esteem. Successful, sophisticated, active "take-charge" people. Value image as an expression of taste.
    2.  *Thinkers (Fulfilleds):* Principles-oriented, high resources. Mature, satisfied, reflective. Value order, functionality, durability.
    3.  *Believers:* Principles-oriented, modest resources. Conservative, conventional, traditional. Favor established brands.
    4.  *Achievers:* Status-oriented, high resources. Work-oriented, conventional, risk-averse. Favor established products that demonstrate success to peers.
    5.  *Strivers:* Status-oriented, low resources. Unsure, impulsive, approval-seeking. Money defines success, emulate high-resource groups.
    6.  *Experiencers:* Action-oriented, high resources. Young, enthusiastic, rebellious, impulsive. Seek variety, spend heavily on fashion, music, and social activities.
    7.  *Makers:* Action-oriented, modest resources. Practical, self-sufficient, hands-on (building, fixing). Unimpressed by status symbols.
    8.  *Survivors (Strugglers):* Lowest resources. Constrained lives, focused on safety and urgent daily needs. Loyal to favorite brands.

#### 3. Applications of Lifestyle Marketing
*   **Positioning New Products:** Identifying a specific lifestyle niche (e.g., positioning a premium home-decor brand specifically toward *Thinkers* who value durability and clean aesthetic, or *Innovators* seeking exclusive designs).
*   **Repositioning Existing Products:** Shifting a brand's image to match changing consumer lifestyles (e.g., repositioning a traditional tea brand as a wellness/green tea lifestyle product).
*   **Media Selection:** Aligning promotional media with psychographic profiles (e.g., advertising high-end outdoor sports gear on digital adventure platforms targeted at *Experiencers*).
*   **Indian Context & Social Influences:** Online shopping patterns vary by social class (upper/middle class buying lifestyle items vs. lower class buying essentials) and family life cycle stages (young singles/newlyweds shopping online for gadgets and apparel vs. older nests prioritizing family/health expenses).

### UNIT 3: ORGANISATIONAL BUYING BEHAVIOUR

#### 1. Consumer vs. Organizational Buying Behaviour
*   **Market Structure:** Organizational markets contain fewer but larger buyers, who are geographically concentrated. Demand is *derived* (dependent on consumer demand) and highly volatile.
*   **Buying Center Complexity:** Consumer buying involves one or a few family members; organizational buying is a formal, multi-person activity involving a **Buying Center**.
*   **Decision Length & Process:** Organizational decisions are highly formalized, follow strict administrative procedures, involve long negotiation cycles, and require detailed contracts.

#### 2. The Buying Center Roles
The **Buying Center** is an informal cross-functional group involved in the purchase decision:
*   *Users:* Members who will actually use the product (e.g., factory technicians).
*   *Influencers:* Set technical specifications or provide evaluative criteria (e.g., consultants).
*   *Deciders:* Have the formal or informal power to make the final choice (e.g., purchase committee).
*   *Buyers:* Have formal authority to select the supplier and arrange commercial terms (e.g., purchase manager).
*   *Gatekeepers:* Control the flow of information to other members (e.g., receptionists, assistants).
*   *Specifiers:* Draft and finalize the technical specifications of the product/service.

#### 3. The Buy Grid Model
Developed by Robinson, Faris, and Wind, it is a matrix crossing **3 Buy Classes** with **8 Buy Phases**:

| Buy Phase / Stage | Straight Re-buy | Modified Re-buy | New Task |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Problem Recognition** | Automatic/Routine | Moderate Review | Detailed Analysis |
| **2. Determination of Characteristics** | Standardized | Some Changes | Thorough Research |
| **3. Description of Specifications** | Pre-established | Minor Edits | Custom Drafted |
| **4. Supplier Search** | Skipped | Limited Search | Extensive Search |
| **5. Acquisition of Proposals** | None | Few Quotes | Detailed RFPs |
| **6. Evaluation & Selection** | Automated | Competitive Bidding | Multi-stage Committee |
| **7. Order-Routine Selection** | Automated | Semi-automated | Custom Terms |
| **8. Performance Feedback** | Routine Check | Occasional Review | Formal Evaluation |

*   *Straight Re-buy:* Low risk, routine purchase (e.g., reordering office stationery).
*   *Modified Re-buy:* Moderate risk, involves changing specs, prices, or suppliers (e.g., upgrading office laptops).
*   *New Task:* High risk, first-time purchase requiring extensive research (e.g., installing a new ERP system).

#### 4. Fisher's Model of Organizational Buying Behaviour
Classifies buying decisions along two dimensions: **Product Complexity** (technical nature) and **Commercial Uncertainty** (financial risk).
*   *Cell 1 (Low Complexity, Low Uncertainty):* Purchase department has dominant influence (Buyer Emphasis). Decisions are routine.
*   *Cell 2 (High Complexity, Low Uncertainty):* Technical experts, engineers, and designers dominate. Focus is on product specs.
*   *Cell 3 (Low Complexity, High Uncertainty):* Financial officers and top management dominate. Focus is on commercial terms/cost.
*   *Cell 4 (High Complexity, High Uncertainty):* Joint decision-making by a multi-disciplinary committee including top executives, technicians, and finance heads.

#### 5. Major Influences on Organizational Buying
Webster and Wind classify these into four levels:
1.  **Environmental Factors:** Economic conditions, political landscape (e.g., *Atmanirbhar Bharat* policy favoring domestic manufacturers), legal regulations, technological advances, infrastructure (e.g., power shortages affecting choice of elevators), and cultural norms.
2.  **Organisational Factors:** Company objectives, formal structures, technology in use, administrative procedures, and resource constraints.
3.  **Interpersonal Factors:** Power dynamics, authority, status, and persuasiveness among buying center members. Conflict resolution methods include *Problem-Solving* and *Persuasion* (rational), or *Bargaining* and *Politicking* (non-rational).
4.  **Individual Factors / Perceived Risk:** Age, income, position, and risk tolerance of decision-makers. Perceived risk is managed depending on:
    *   *Problem characteristics:* Expense value, novelty of the task, and product essentiality.

#### 6. Illustrative Application: Purchase of CCTV Cameras
*   **Individual Purchase (Consumer):** An individual homeowner wants a CCTV camera. The buying center is tiny (usually self/spouse). The process is fast (1-3 days), informal, and based on brand image or online search. There is no technical spec sheet or formal contract.
*   **Organizational Purchase (B2B):** A bank wants to install CCTV systems across 500 branches. The Buying Center is large (IT head sets specs, security officer evaluates views, finance manager negotiates, purchase manager executes contract). It is a formal, multi-week process involving RFP submissions, site testing, warranty negotiations, and annual maintenance service contracts.
*   *Fisher's Model alignment:* For the bank, this represents high product complexity and high commercial uncertainty (Cell 4), requiring executive committee approval.

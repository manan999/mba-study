### UNIT 9: REFERENCE GROUP INFLUENCE AND GROUP DYNAMICS

#### 1. Concept of Reference Groups and Types
*   **Definition:** Any person or group that serves as a point of comparison (or reference) for an individual in forming general or specific values, attitudes, or behavior.
*   **Types of Reference Groups:**
    1.  *Membership vs. Non-Membership:* Groups to which an individual belongs vs. those they do not belong to but are influenced by.
    2.  *Formal vs. Informal:* Defined structure, rules, and list of members (e.g., a professional association) vs. loose, unstructured groups based on friendship/interests (e.g., a peer group, neighbors).
    3.  *Primary vs. Secondary:* Frequent, face-to-face, intimate interaction (e.g., family, close friends) vs. infrequent, structured, impersonal contact (e.g., a professional club, shopping group).
    4.  *Aspirational Groups:* Groups an individual desires/aspires to join. Classified as:
        *   *Anticipatory:* Groups one expects to join in the future (e.g., junior executives aspiring to join the senior management club).
        *   *Symbolic:* Groups one admires but is unlikely to ever join (e.g., professional sports teams). Marketers use celebrities to leverage symbolic aspiration.
    5.  *Dissociative (Negative) Groups:* Groups with which an individual wishes to avoid association (e.g., avoiding clothing styles associated with a group they are trying to leave).

#### 2. Types of Reference Group Influences
*   **Normative (Utilitarian) Influence:** Encourages conformity to group norms to gain rewards or avoid punishment/social rejection (e.g., dressing appropriately for a corporate dinner).
*   **Comparative (Value-Expressive) Influence:** Comparing oneself to others to validate personal beliefs and attitudes, using the group to express self-identity (e.g., smoking in groups where it is considered "cool").
*   **Informational Influence:** Accepting information from others because they are perceived as credible, expert sources (e.g., asking a tech-savvy friend for laptop recommendations).

#### 3. Conspicuousness Matrix: Influence on Product vs. Brand Choice
Reference group influence depends on a product's *Conspicuousness* (defined by visibility and exclusivity):

| Dimension | Public (Visible Consumption) | Private (Hidden Consumption) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Luxury (Exclusive)** | **Public Luxuries** (e.g., Premium Smartphone, Fashion Clothing)<br>• *Product Influence:* Strong<br>• *Brand Influence:* Strong | **Private Luxuries** (e.g., Home Theater System, Espresso Maker)<br>• *Product Influence:* Strong<br>• *Brand Influence:* Weak |
| **Necessity (Owned by all)** | **Public Necessities** (e.g., Wristwatch, Automobile)<br>• *Product Influence:* Weak<br>• *Brand Influence:* Strong | **Private Necessities** (e.g., Floor Cleaner, Charcoal Water Filter)<br>• *Product Influence:* Weak<br>• *Brand Influence:* Weak |

*   *Application Examples:*
    *   *Fashion Clothing:* Public necessity. High visibility, low exclusivity. Reference groups strongly influence the *brand* choice (e.g., Zara vs. H&M) but not the product class.
    *   *Mobile Phone:* Public luxury/necessity. High visibility, high personal identity association. Reference groups strongly influence both *product and brand* (e.g., iPhone as a status symbol).
    *   *Life Insurance:* Private necessity. Low visibility, high complexity. No peer-group pressure; buying is based on financial security needs.
    *   *Charcoal Water Filter:* Private necessity. Hidden in the kitchen, low social visibility. Buying is based purely on functional utility and cost, not reference group pressure.

#### 4. Bases of Social Power
1.  *Reward Power:* Ability to provide rewards (e.g., peer group acceptance for wearing trendy clothes).
2.  *Coercive Power:* Ability to threaten or withhold rewards (e.g., ads warning of social embarrassment due to bad breath or body odor, pushing mouthwash/deodorant purchases).
3.  *Legitimate Power:* Power derived from cultural/group values (e.g., professional dress codes).
4.  *Referent Power:* Power based on the consumer's admiration of or desire to identify with the group/individual (e.g., celebrity endorsements).
5.  *Expert Power:* Power derived from possessing specialized knowledge (e.g., doctors endorsing toothpastes).

#### 5. Opinion Leadership
*   **Definition:** The process by which one person (the opinion leader) informally influences the actions or attitudes of others (opinion seekers/recipients).
*   **Key Characteristics:** Opinion leaders are product-specific, highly involved in the product category, read specialized media, have high self-confidence, are socially active, and are perceived as objective/neutral sources.
*   **Transmission Models:**
    *   *Two-Step Flow:* Media $\to$ Opinion Leaders $\to$ General Public.
    *   *Multi-Step Flow:* Interactive, multi-directional flow between Media, Opinion Leaders, Gatekeepers, and Opinion Receivers.

#### 6. Social Class Concept and Marketing Applications
*   **Definition:** Relatively permanent and homogeneous divisions in society into which individuals or families sharing similar values, lifestyles, interests, and behavior can be categorized.
*   **Indicators (Warner's ISC):** Measured using socio-economic composite variables:
    1. *Occupation* (most important) | 2. *Source of Income* (inherited vs. salary) | 3. *House Type* | 4. *Dwelling Area*
*   **Consumer Choices by Social Class:**
    *   *Housing:* Upper classes value privacy and prestige (exclusive gated estates). Middle classes prioritize safety, connectivity, and standard amenities (suburban apartments). Lower classes focus on basic shelter and affordability.
    *   *Holidays:* Upper classes seek exotic, customized, self-enriching international travel. Middle classes prefer standard family package tours and domestic destinations. Working classes prioritize visiting relatives or local religious pilgrimages.
    *   *Banking Services:* Upper classes demand personalized wealth management, private banking, and premium credit cards. Middle classes focus on retail banking, home/car loans, and mutual fund savings (SIPs). Lower classes utilize basic zero-balance accounts and micro-loans.

### UNIT 10: FAMILY BUYING INFLUENCES, FAMILY LIFE CYCLE AND BUYING ROLES

#### 1. The Family as a Social Group
*   **Why a Social Group?** The family is a primary group characterized by intimate, face-to-face interaction, shared values, and high emotional bonding, serving as the most basic unit of consumption and socialization.

#### 2. Traditional Family Life Cycle (FLC) Stages
1.  *Bachelor Stage:* Young, single. Low earnings but high discretionary income. Spends on fashion, gadgets, travel, and online food ordering.
2.  *Newly Married:* Young couple, no children. High expenditure period focused on home set-up, appliances, consumer electronics, and furniture.
3.  *Full Nest 1:* Young married, youngest child under 6. Income drops if one partner stops working. High spending on baby food, toys, baby medicine, and pediatric care.
4.  *Full Nest 2:* Older married, youngest child over 6. Financial position improves (career progress, dual income). Spends on education, children's clothes, bicycles, and family foods.
5.  *Full Nest 3:* Older married, dependent children. High income but experienced, value-conscious buyers. High education and replacement expenditures.
6.  *Empty Nest:* Older married, no children at home. Peak savings and financial stability. Spends on travel, home improvements, luxury appliances (e.g., dishwashers), and medical care.
7.  *Solitary Survivor:* Older, single, retired. Drastic drop in income. Spends mainly on health care, security, and simple maintenance services.
*   *Note:* The *Modern FLC* accounts for non-traditional setups (single parents, childless couples, dual-income-no-kids/DINKs) who demand time-saving convenience goods.

#### 3. Joint Decision-Making in Families
Decisions are categorized into four styles:
1.  *Husband-Dominant:* Husband makes final decision (e.g., life insurance, car, financial investments).
2.  *Wife-Dominant:* Wife makes final decision (e.g., groceries, kitchen appliances, home furnishings, kids' daily wear).
3.  *Joint / Syncratic:* Both make the decision together (e.g., buying a house, school for children, family vacation).
4.  *Autonomic:* Either partner makes the purchase independently (e.g., personal care, casual books, routine office wear).

#### 4. Consumer Socialization of Children
*   **Definition:** The process by which young people acquire the skills, knowledge, and attitudes relevant to their functioning as consumers.
*   **Socializing Agents:** Parents (teach rational, budget-oriented consumption), peers (teach brand preferences and social conformity), and television/digital media (drive emotional/hedonic desires).
*   **Learning Processes:**
    *   *Imitation/Modeling:* Observing and copying parental consumption habits (leading to multi-generational brand loyalty).
    *   *Operant Conditioning:* Praise/reward for good buying choices (e.g., finishing healthy meals) vs. disapproval of wasteful habits.
    *   *Financial Autonomy:* Learning to manage pocket money, allowances, and gifts.

### UNIT 11: CULTURE AND SUBCULTURAL INFLUENCES

#### 1. Concept and Characteristics of Culture
*   **Definition:** A complex set of values, ideas, beliefs, attitudes, and symbols created by humans to shape behavior, transmitted from one generation to the next.
*   **Components:**
    *   *Internal Mental Culture:* Cognitive (ideas, beliefs about religion/life) and Normative (values, rules, moral codes).
    *   *External Material Culture:* Visual/tangible things (art, technology, clothing, housing).
*   **Characteristics:** Culture is *invented*, *learned* (via patterned instruction and imitation), *shared* (via family, school, media), *gratifying & persistent*, *dynamic & adaptive*, *integrated*, *prescriptive*, and *similar yet different* across societies.

#### 2. Indian Sub-cultures and Consumption Patterns
Sub-cultures are homogeneous subgroups within a heterogeneous national culture:
*   **Religious Sub-cultures:** (e.g., Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs, Jains, Christians).
    *   *Consumption Mores:* Festivals (Diwali, Eid, Christmas) trigger heavy buying of clothes, jewelry, and gifts.
    *   *Taboos:* Halal foods for Muslims, vegetarianism/no root vegetables for Jains, and beef restrictions for Hindus.
*   **Geographical/Regional Sub-cultures:** (e.g., Bengali, Punjabi, Tamil, Gujarati).
    *   *Food Habits:* South India prefers rice, coconut oil, and filter coffee. North India prefers wheat, dairy (ghee/butter), and tea. East India prefers mustard oil and fish.
    *   *Clothing:* Silk saris are highly preferred in the South (Kanchipuram), cottons in the hot North-West, and woolens are seasonal in the North.
    *   *Language:* 22 official regional languages require localized, translated marketing communications.

#### 3. Cross-Cultural Consumer Behaviour
*   **Standardization (Global Approach):** Selling the same product using the same marketing campaign worldwide (e.g., luxury watch brands, iPhones). Assumes consumer needs are homogeneous.
*   **Localization (Adaptive Approach):** Adapting the product and promotional strategies to local cultural values (e.g., McDonald's McAloo Tikki in India, Kellogg's adjusting cereals for hot milk consumption in Indian households).
